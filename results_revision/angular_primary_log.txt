PRE-SPECIFICATION (stated before fitting)
  Primary predictor summary : angular disparity at trial ONSET (angDisp_onset_deg)
  Secondary                 : time-weighted mean angular disparity (angDisp_mean_deg)
  Primary response          : angular response displacement at the onset head pose

  Why onset is primary:
   (i)   Exogeneity. The onset geometry follows from the stimulus placement and
         from whatever pose the listener happens to hold when the trial starts.
         It is not shaped by where the listener subsequently chooses to walk.
         The time-weighted mean is a function of the trajectory, and the
         trajectory may itself be driven by the percept under study, so a slope
         on it mixes a stimulus effect with self-selected sampling.
   (ii)  Frame consistency. An angular response measure must be evaluated from
         some head position. Onset is the only pose available on every trial
         that is independent of the response, so predictor and response are
         defined in one and the same geometric frame.
   (iii) Comparability. Onset angular disparity is the quantity the
         fixed-listener ventriloquism literature manipulates, so it places the
         present slope on the same axis as prior work.
  The time-weighted mean is reported alongside throughout, since it is the
  summary that embodies the 'changes over time' half of the reviewer's point.

STEP 1: ANGULAR RESPONSE DISPLACEMENT
==============================================================================
Let H be a head position and u_s, u_c, u_r the unit vectors from H towards
the true source, the visual cue and the response placement. Let t be the unit
vector in the (u_s, u_c) plane, orthogonal to u_s and pointing towards u_c:

   angular disparity      theta_D = atan2(|u_s x u_c|, u_s . u_c)
   angular response shift theta_R = atan2(u_r . t,     u_r . u_s)

theta_R is 0 deg when the response lies in the source direction and equals
theta_D when it lies in the cue direction, so theta_R / theta_D is the angular
analogue of the bias proportion and d(theta_R)/d(theta_D) the angular analogue
of the published displacement slope. The out-of-plane component of the response
direction is discarded, exactly as the published signed error discards the
orthogonal error component.

Three evaluation frames are computed: the first logged head pose (onset,
primary), the last logged head pose (sensitivity), and the time-weighted
average over all samples of the trial (matched partner of the time-weighted
mean angular disparity).
